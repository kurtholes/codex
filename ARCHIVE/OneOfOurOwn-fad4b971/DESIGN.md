# ONE OF OUR OWN
Inspiration image: fad4b971

A first-person detective action sandbox: inhabit the oppressive green precinct, put physical proof beneath the interrogation lamp, then burst out into its alleys to catch the officer who runs. The core verb is **investigate by doing**: handle objects, scan traces, confront bodies, pursue and restrain.

Loop: take a photographic assignment → scan a physical trace → carry proof to the steel table → expose the culprit → chase and arrest → invest in the precinct.

The room is the anchor: detailed scratched metal table, bent folding chairs, seated charcoal-uniformed officers, paper notices, harsh overhead light and deep green shadows. A continuous precinct and courtyard has breakable glass, loose furniture, service passages and accessible upper walkways. Icons and glowing physical objects guide the first action from the starting position. No dialogue or mandatory text.

Main shifts persist credits and upgrades. Four branches each have six tiers: forensics (scan reach, reward and luminous tool rings), pursuit (speed, stamina and vault height), restraint (arrest reach, stronger stun and blue cuff hardware), facilities (income, light fixtures and access to the upper gallery and motor court). Tier prices rise for a long progression, with each tier altering equipment or environment. Quick shift supplies capable tools immediately, randomizes the culprit and evidence, and ends with a compact performance score after one arrest, without a clock limit.

Minute 1: lift a bright evidence pouch, slam it into the lamp cone, watch a guilty officer spring up, chase through a shattering partition and cuff him. Minute 10: chain randomized cases, learn shortcuts, break furniture to cut corners and tune four upgrade branches. Minute 30: powerful scanning and restraint equipment, much faster traversal, unlocked upper routes and increasingly profitable shifts support an uninterrupted flow of cases.

Action feedback: procedural footsteps, scan pulses, steel clacks, chair scrapes, impact bursts, camera kick, brief hit-stop and large payout numbers. A collateral counter gives careful players a competing mastery goal. Low/medium/high presets control shadows, pixel ratio and debris. Instanced debris keeps destruction cheap. Source code contains a development-only test API; the static release strips it.

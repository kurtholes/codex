# OVERBOARD
Inspiration image: 0869347a

## One-page design
**Fantasy / verb.** A tiny swimmer inside towering honey-colored swells. Swim, grab, lash, steer. The sea is the playground: rise onto a crest, skim toward a glinting crate, then climb onto a creaking raft that becomes your own moving island.

**Genre.** Third-person ocean survival sandbox, with an instant one-life rescue arcade mode. Start in motion beside a reachable plank. No dialogue, gates, or quests. A floating hand and compact control hints teach the interaction.

**Loop.** Ride swells → recover physical wreckage → lash a stronger, larger raft → sail farther → smash and salvage larger wrecks.

**World.** An uninterrupted procedural amber ocean. Visible landmarks include a sheltered reef, ship graveyard, storm belt, and volcanic shallows. Buoyant salvage, broken hulls, and a passing rescue ship make silhouettes worth pursuing. Exposure rises in water; stamina powers bursts and recovers aboard. Failure returns the main-mode swimmer to their raft and spills some salvage; quick-play ends after one life or rescue.

**Progression.** Six connected disciplines, each with five permanent tiers: lashings (deck footprint and strength), fins (swim speed and dolphin bursts), rigging (mast, sail area, steering speed), hold (physical cargo and exposure resistance), workshop (windmill, salvage multipliers, reinforced bow), depth (bell, diving reach, compact sub and underwater travel). Costs climb through 30 purchases; levels visibly enlarge decks, add barrels/rails, tall sails, cargo, turbines, and a bell/sub. Hull level gates advanced sail and workshop tiers; storage supports depth equipment. New movement makes distant waters practical without invisible barriers. No prestige reset. Build menu remains playable with the sea visible.

**Feel.** Shader-displaced waves with world-space triplanar grain, sun reflection, trough fog, spray, camera bob and droplets. A detailed articulated swimmer strokes through water; a multi-plank raft pitches with the surface. Grabs trigger gold splinters, rising numbers, layered synthesized sounds and a short camera impulse. Ram wrecks to scatter salvage; fragile decks lose loose cargo in storms.

**Minute 1.** Catch the first crate, climb aboard, lash the first wider deck. **Minute 10.** Sail and towline make wreck hunting fast; larger smashable hulks shower the sea with resources. **Minute 30.** A large sail fortress with wind workshop and submersible makes the outer sea a high-speed salvage route.

**Quick-play.** A 150-second rescue sprint, randomized wreckage and vessel bearing. Reach the moving rescue ship before exposure or time runs out. Supplies buy run-only upgrades; the route rewards swimming bursts, rafting, and risky wreck smashing.

**Implementation scope.** Kinematic buoyancy and modular construction, not a general rigid-body solver. Procedural repeated sea regions provide free roam; landmarks and wrecks are finite handcrafted procedural clusters. Deep equipment changes are systemic speed/range/strength upgrades with visible assemblies rather than separate simulated interiors.
